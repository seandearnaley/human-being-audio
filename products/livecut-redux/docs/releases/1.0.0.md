# Livecut Redux 1.0.0

Livecut Redux is a live beat slicer for breaks: it re-edits the audio passing
through it into phrases of cuts and repeats, in time with the host. It is a
ground-up rewrite of Remy Muller's Livecut (2003 and 2004), which was only
ever released as a 32-bit plug-in and no longer loads.

Version 1.0.0 introduced the name Livecut Redux. Up to 1.0.0-beta.1 it was called Livecut. It is the first full release
since 0.2.0, and it plays from an XY pad.

## Installing the current version

[Get Livecut Redux from Human Being Audio](https://humanbeingaudio.com/#livecut-redux). The following table records the original 1.0.0 packages.

| | Download | What it installs |
|---|---|---|
| **macOS** 11 or later, Apple Silicon and Intel | `Livecut-Redux-1.0.0.pkg`, signed and notarised | The VST3 and Audio Unit in `/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins`, and the standalone app in `/Applications` |
| **Windows** 10 or later, x64 | `Livecut-Redux-1.0.0-windows-x64.zip` | Copy `Livecut Redux.vst3` to `C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3`. The standalone app runs from anywhere |

The plug-in appears in the host as **Livecut Redux**.

The macOS installer first removes Livecut 0.1.0 to 1.0.0-beta.1 from
`/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins` and `/Applications`, because Livecut Redux has the
same plug-in IDs and a host would otherwise list both. It leaves anything
else called Livecut alone, including the 2004 original. If you copied an
earlier Livecut into your own `~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins`, or onto Windows,
remove that copy yourself.

## The pad

New instances open on a pad. The legacy pages are a click away, under
**Classic**.

- **Press** to engage. Cutting starts on the next unit of the program's grid.
- **Let go** and the cuts carry on to the next beat or bar, set by **Beat / Bar**,
  then fade to the input over 5 ms. Program switches land on the same grid.
- **Drag** to play two settings of the program at once. Each axis is named and
  valued on a tag in a corner of the pad.
- **Hold** latches the pad. The latch survives loop wraps, seeks and stop/play.
- **Straight** keeps every cut on even divisions of the beat, so a program plays
  no triplets or other tuplets.
- **Mix** blends the input with the program.
- Each program has a fixed seed and a fixed neutral base, so the same spot in
  the same bar always sounds the same.

## The eight programs

| Program | X | Y | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cut** | Density: 8ths to 32nds, with more repeats | Departure: from near the original order to a full re-edit | CutProc11. The bottom-left is nearly the untouched break, the top-right busy jungle editing. |
| **Bounce** | Triplet feel, with halftime at the far right | Ratchet climb | Triplet bounces against a straight beat, and ratchets that get faster and rise in pitch to close each phrase. |
| **Glitch** | Repeat rate: 1/16 to 1/128 | Pitch step per repeat, falling to rising | SQPusher's fills, through a crusher that gets crunchier as the repeats get faster. |
| **Suction** | Where the band sits, 120 Hz to 5 kHz | How narrow and resonant it is | The cuts squeeze into a singing band that bursts 1½ octaves open on every cut and sucks shut behind it over 180 ms. Level is held, so squeezing never thins the sound. |
| **Filter** | A low-pass from 200 Hz to fully open | How much it rings | The end of every roll strikes a tail on the cutoff's own note. The tail ducks 12 dB under each hit, so the hits stay clean and the tail sings in the gaps. |
| **Tape** | Stop length: ½, 1, 2 or 4 beats | How often: every 4 bars up to every beat | The track plays untouched, brakes to a halt on the grid, then spins back up and lands exactly back on the track. |
| **Loops** | Loop length: a bar down to a beat | Depth | Captures the bar you press in and loops it, folding into shorter loops of its first hit. No shorter loop is louder than the loop it sits in. |
| **Rush** | Drag or rush | Roll length: 1 to 4 beats | The track plays until the last beats of each bar, then a roll repeats that beat's hit into the downbeat: evenly, rushing and rising up to a fourth, or dragging and falling. |

## The editor

- **White on black.** The only colour is a program's: the pad's sheet, the name
  on its tab, and the block being cut on the phrase tape.
- **A picture for each program**, drawn as vectors every frame. It shows what
  the program does, the pad's two axes move it the way they move the sound,
  and while the pad cuts it keeps the host's beat. Each new block of cuts
  kicks it.
- **The phrase as a strip of tape** across the top, cut into its bars, with the
  block being cut in the program's colour. Until there is a phrase, the tape's
  place says what to do next.
- **A waiting program glows on each beat** until its switch lands.
- **An About sheet**, opened from the wordmark, with the plug-in's story.
- **The Classic pages on one grid**, with the same controls as the pad.
- The axis sliders sit along the pad's bottom and right edges, so the keyboard
  and screen readers reach each axis.

[docs/skin.md](../skin.md) has the design rules.

## Clean by design

- Between its stops, rolls and loops, Tape, Rush and Loops play the input
  sample for sample.
- Joins, switches and restarts crossfade: 2 ms inside Rush's rolls and Loops'
  folds, 5 ms when a switch or restart cuts a sound short.
- Switching **Surface** between Classic and Pad while playing doesn't click.
  Classic's cuts release with Classic's own settings, and Classic's first cut
  after an idle pad fades in.

## Classic

The beta left open whether the legacy pages, Studio and Spectral would stay.
They all stay. Classic holds the four cut procedures with every one of their
controls, and everything built between 0.2.0 and the pad. 0.3.0 to 0.8.0 were
never published, so this is their first release:

- **Capture**: keep the last complete phrase and save it as a stereo float WAV;
  Wet and Trim ([0.3.0](0.3.0.md)).
- **Perform**: Audio Hold, Swing and Accent ([0.4.0](0.4.0.md)).
- **Recut**: Studio's 500 ms look-ahead, Attack Align and Reverse
  ([0.5.1](0.5.1.md), [guide](0.5.0.md)).
- **Impact**: Slice Filter, Drive / Wavefold and a Transient Shaper
  ([0.6.0](0.6.0.md)).
- **Fracture**: the GrainCut and TapeCut readers, and Ring / Frequency Shift
  ([0.7.0](0.7.0.md)).
- **Resonance**: a four-voice tuned resonator, a choice of effect order, and
  Spectral Freeze / Smear ([0.8.0](0.8.0.md)).

## Since 1.0.0-beta.1

- The plug-in name changed to Livecut Redux.
- The editor's new look, and its art keeping the host's beat.
- The Classic pages' layout. Each status now sits beside the control it
  describes, and slider ends meet their captions.
- The line above Classic's tape ("Bar 2 of 4") is gone; the tape shows it,
  and screen readers still read it.
- No change to the sound, the parameters or the saved state.

## Compatibility

- The plug-in IDs are unchanged, so sessions saved with Livecut 0.1.0, 0.2.0
  and 1.0.0-beta.1 open in Livecut Redux.
- The pad parameters are appended with version hint 9. No existing parameter's
  index, ID, range or choices change, so automation from 0.2.0 still lands on
  the same controls.
- The state schema is 9. A session saved before the pad opens on **Classic** and
  sounds the same as before. New instances open on the pad.

## Known limits

- **The Windows builds are not code-signed.** Windows may ask before it runs
  the standalone app.
- **Studio on the pad.** A session set to Studio on the Classic pages keeps its
  500 ms look-ahead on the pad, so touches land 500 ms late. Set it to Live to
  play the pad.
- **The art always moves.** JUCE can't read the system's reduce-motion setting.
- Each program's own limits are listed in its design spec under
  `docs/superpowers/specs/`, and each Classic feature's in its release notes
  above.

## Validation

All 758 tests pass on macOS and Windows CI:
- 264 processor, UI and state cases;
- 494 engine cases.

CI also runs [pluginval](https://github.com/Tracktion/pluginval) at strictness
10 on the VST3 on both platforms, and `auval` on the Audio Unit. Pinned output
hashes keep Classic and every program bit-identical across changes, with
separate values for Apple Clang and MSVC. Each program, the new look and the
beat lock had an independent review.

The plug-in was played in Ableton Live 12 on an Apple Silicon Mac. The Windows
builds were not played by hand, and VoiceOver and Narrator were not run.
